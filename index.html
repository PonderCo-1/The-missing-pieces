THE MISSING PIECES

Disappearance of Amelia Earhart: Sources

Status as of October 10, 2026. No wreckage or remains have been confirmed. Every theory in the video is labelled as a hypothesis. The Taraia Object expedition was still at sea when this was written, so check the latest results.

1. The final flight and the radio log

Primary recordRadio Log of the Last Communications of Amelia Earhart, U.S. National Archives

Supports: the Itasca's role, the call sign, and the last message at about 8:43 a.m., "we are on the line 157 337". Also the largest air and sea search of its time.

Magazine1937 Fifty Years Ago, American Heritage (July/August 1987)

Supports: Miami departure on June 1, the 2,556-mile Lae-to-Howland leg, Howland's size, the final messages, and the search of about 250,000 square miles by a carrier, a battleship, four destroyers and a minesweeper. Its view is that the plane most likely ran out of fuel at sea.

News featureNew Clues in 70-year-old Earhart Mystery, Associated Press (2007), via Aviation Pros

Supports: the 7:42 a.m. message, Fred Noonan's earlier work at Pan American Airways, the 1940 discovery of bones on Gardner Island, and the TIGHAR castaway thesis. Note: it gives 8:55 a.m. for the last message, a different clock from the National Archives.

Secondary2 July 1937, This Day in Aviation

Supports: takeoff at about 10:00 a.m. local time. The note about clouds blocking star sights comes from a reply by the site author, so treat it as a secondary claim.

2. Official explanation and declassified files

News reportAmelia Earhart records released by U.S., CBS News (Nov 17, 2025)

Supports: the declassified National Archives release, the Itasca log's "Earhart Unheard" entries, the 1960 Saipan newspaper story, the prevailing "ran out of fuel" belief, and experts' doubt that the files change the story.

News reportSonar image was not Amelia Earhart's plane, CBS News (Nov 26, 2024)

Supports: the 2024 Deep Sea Vision sonar image later identified as a natural rock formation.

3. The Nikumaroro (castaway) hypothesis

Peer-reviewed abstractJantz, "Amelia Earhart and the Nikumaroro Bones", Forensic Anthropology (2018)

Supports: the 1940 bones, the Fiji doctor's sex assessment, and the finding that Earhart is more similar to the bones than 99% of individuals in a reference sample. I read the abstract only, not the full paper. The author is part of the group that advances this hypothesis.

News reportResearchers announce new effort to find Amelia Earhart's plane, NBC News (July 2025)

Supports: Pettigrew's claims about the Taraia Object, the tools and medicine vial, the 2017 dog search, TIGHAR's dozen expeditions, and Gillespie's view that the object is a palm root ball.

4. The Japanese-capture claim and the 2017 photo

MagazineSmithsonian Curator Weighs In on Photo That Allegedly Shows Amelia Earhart in Japanese Captivity, Smithsonian Magazine (2017)

Supports: the History Channel claim, the curator's caution, the blogger's discovery of an earlier travel-book source, and that no confirmed trace has been found.

ReferenceAmelia Earhart: The Lost Evidence, Wikipedia

Supports: the 1935 publication date of the photo and the 4.3 million viewers (which cites USA Today's Nielsen report; I did not open that report).

5. The Taraia Object expedition

InstitutionEarhart expedition update: Crew at sea, expected to arrive on Nikumaroro in 72 hours, Purdue University (Oct 9, 2026)

Supports: the voyage progress, the expected Oct 12 arrival, the object first noticed in 2020 and visible in 1938 aerial photos, and the three-stage plan (imaging, magnetometer and sonar, hydraulic dredge).

Institution1 month out: Countdown to the search for Amelia Earhart's plane begins, Purdue University (Oct 2025)

Supports: Earhart's two years at Purdue and the Purdue Research Foundation's funding of her Electra.

News reportAmelia Earhart mystery could finally be solved after 90 years, LADbible (Oct 9, 2026)

Supports: the expedition status and its stated goal of identifying the object. Used only as a secondary report of the Purdue updates.
